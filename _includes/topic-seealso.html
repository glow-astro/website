{%- comment -%} The "See also" block at the foot of every topic page: the work-package tasks the topic feeds, and the papers behind it. Restructured 2026-09-07, at the owner's word. It used to be a two-column grid, one cell for the work packages and one for the papers, each a run of links separated by middots. That reads at two papers and fails at twenty: the tasks are capped at three by the programme and will stay so, the papers are not capped by anything, so one cell grows without bound beside one that never does. The two are now stacked bands of equal width, and the papers are a list, one per line, so the block grows downward instead of sideways. The list is capped at six, with the remainder pointing at the publications page. The cap is the point: a topic page should say what the work rests on, not become a second copy of the publications list. Order is the topic's own `papers` list, verbatim, and the cap takes the top of it. The list was briefly sorted by date instead, which was wrong (owner, 2026-09-16): if six of twenty are going to be shown, they have to be the six that matter most to the topic, and only the person who curates the list knows which those are. A date sort silently discards that judgement and keeps whatever is newest. An id with no matching paper is caught by the data-integrity check in docs/SITE_CONVENTIONS.md §3, and skipped here rather than rendered blank. This include carries the whole block, markup included, because the six topic pages held an identical copy of it until today and identical copies drift. Takes: include.topic (a _data/topics.yml entry). {%- endcomment -%} {%- assign total = include.topic.papers | size -%} {%- assign limit = 6 -%}
