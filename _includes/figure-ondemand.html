WHEN TO USE THIS INSTEAD OF AN AUTOPLAYING LOOP Use it when the animation is a sequence with a beginning and an end -- a derivation, a sweep through a parameter, anything with deliberate pauses in it. A reader who arrives mid-sweep on such a figure sees a frame that means nothing, and a loop restarts the argument under them while they are still reading the caption. Use the autoplaying loop (`topic-figure.html`) when the motion is a texture rather than a story and any frame is as good as any other. THE POSTER MUST BE THE LAST FRAME This is what makes the pattern work rather than merely quiet. With the last frame as the poster, the figure already shows the finished result before anyone plays it, and settles back onto exactly that picture when it ends: press play and you return to where you started. With the first frame as the poster you get a figure that says nothing until played and then rests on something different from how it began, which reads as a stuck video. Most generators in ~/code/application_plots poster somewhere in the middle by default, so the frame usually has to be pulled out of the encoded video: ffmpeg -sseof -0.1 -i media/.webm -frames:v 1 media/_still.png ACCESSIBILITY `controls` is the affordance, and it is why assets/js/reduced-motion.js does not touch this: that script rewrites `video[autoplay]`, and nothing here moves until a reader asks, which is what the setting is for. The `.video-frame` wrapper exists only to hang a play badge over the still, because the browser's own control bar sits at the bottom edge of a wide figure and is easy to read as part of the plot. It is pure CSS and pure decoration: see main.css. `alt` describes the motion for a reader who cannot see it; the caption says what it means. They are different jobs and neither replaces the other -- and because the poster is the whole result, a reader who never presses play still has the figure.
